# Rulebook: Correct Shot Size and Angle Changes

## Main Principle

Every new angle must change at least one of five things: emotion, information, relationship between characters/objects, rhythm, or geography. If a new angle changes nothing, it is unnecessary.

## Hard Prohibitions and Fixes

### NO_CU_TO_ELS_DEFAULT
- Do not: Cut directly from a close-up or extreme close-up to an extreme long shot by default.
- Why: It tears the viewer away from emotional focus and resets geography too violently.
- Fix: Insert a medium or wide bridge, match action, sound bridge, reaction cutaway, or use the wide as a deliberate reveal.
- Exception: Shock, comedy, loneliness reveal, time jump, scale contrast, scene ending, or intentional alienation. Mark it as intentional.

### NO_MICRO_ANGLE_CHANGE
- Do not: Cut between two shots of the same subject from almost the same angle or size unless you want a jump cut.
- Why: Small perspective changes feel like an error, not a new idea.
- Fix: Move at least about 30 degrees, change lens or scale clearly, or cover with an insert/B-roll.
- Exception: Only when it is an intentional stylistic device supported by story, sound, movement, or a clear reset.

### NO_AXIS_CROSS_WITHOUT_RESET
- Do not: Cross the 180-degree line without a reset.
- Why: Screen direction and left/right relationships can flip, confusing the viewer.
- Fix: Use a neutral-on-axis shot, motivated camera move across the line, cutaway, top shot, or new establishing shot.
- Exception: Only when it is an intentional stylistic device supported by story, sound, movement, or a clear reset.

### NO_UNMOTIVATED_CAMERA_MOVE
- Do not: Pan, orbit, gimbal-push, or zoom only because the frame feels static.
- Why: Unmotivated movement looks cheap and distracts from story or product proof.
- Fix: Tie movement to new information, emotion, actor action, product reveal, or shift in power.
- Exception: Only when it is an intentional stylistic device supported by story, sound, movement, or a clear reset.

### NO_EYELINE_BREAK
- Do not: Cut to a reaction or object that violates the viewer's expected eyeline.
- Why: The viewer loses who is looking at what.
- Fix: Rebuild with POV to object to reaction, or use an insert that clarifies target.
- Exception: Only when it is an intentional stylistic device supported by story, sound, movement, or a clear reset.

### NO_SCREEN_POSITION_TELEPORT
- Do not: Move the subject from left side to right side of frame across a cut without motivation.
- Why: The eye has to hunt for the subject and the edit feels clumsy.
- Fix: Match screen position, movement direction, gaze vector, or give a wider reset.
- Exception: Only when it is an intentional stylistic device supported by story, sound, movement, or a clear reset.

### NO_STYLE_MIX_RANDOMLY
- Do not: Mix handheld chaos, locked-off luxury, top-down tutorial, and Dutch angle tension in the same short sequence without hierarchy.
- Why: The viewer reads it as lack of visual strategy.
- Fix: Pick a base grammar and allow one contrast grammar for the turning point.
- Exception: Only when it is an intentional stylistic device supported by story, sound, movement, or a clear reset.

### NO_REVEAL_BEFORE_CONTEXT
- Do not: Cut to a product/solution hero shot before the viewer understands the problem or desire.
- Why: The reveal has no charge.
- Fix: Set up problem, friction, discovery, proof, then hero.
- Exception: Only when it is an intentional stylistic device supported by story, sound, movement, or a clear reset.

### NO_MACRO_WITHOUT_ORIENTATION
- Do not: Start with a macro detail if the object is impossible to identify and mystery is not intended.
- Why: Macro becomes visual noise.
- Fix: Add an identifying insert within 1-2 beats or use audio/text to anchor.
- Exception: Only when it is an intentional stylistic device supported by story, sound, movement, or a clear reset.

### NO_REACTIONLESS_CLAIM
- Do not: Show only product shots for UGC claims; include human/pet reaction or proof context.
- Why: UGC needs trust and behavior, not just beauty frames.
- Fix: Add facecam, owner/pet interaction, screen proof, or side-by-side demo.
- Exception: Only when it is an intentional stylistic device supported by story, sound, movement, or a clear reset.

### NO_FALSE_BEFORE_AFTER
- Do not: Use before/after transformations unless claims are safe, provable, and not misleading.
- Why: Especially in beauty, health, pet, and tech claims, false proof damages trust and can be non-compliant.
- Fix: Use demonstration, routine, testimonial framing, or measurable non-medical result.
- Exception: Only when it is an intentional stylistic device supported by story, sound, movement, or a clear reset.

### NO_OVER_CUTTING_DIALOGUE
- Do not: Cut every phrase or every word in a testimonial/podcast scene.
- Why: It kills authenticity and removes micro-reactions.
- Fix: Cut on thought shifts, breaths, reactions, object inserts, or laughter beats.
- Exception: Only when it is an intentional stylistic device supported by story, sound, movement, or a clear reset.

### NO_LIGHT_DIRECTION_JUMP
- Do not: Cut between shots with conflicting key-light direction/time-of-day if the scene is meant to be continuous.
- Why: The viewer feels a continuity error even when they cannot name it.
- Fix: Use transitional B-roll, motivated location shift, or make the light change a story point.
- Exception: Only when it is an intentional stylistic device supported by story, sound, movement, or a clear reset.

### NO_LENS_LANGUAGE_WHIPLASH
- Do not: Jump from ultra-wide distortion to compressed telephoto portrait without intent.
- Why: Space, face shape, and product proportions change abruptly.
- Fix: Bridge with medium focal length or assign lens shift to POV change.
- Exception: Only when it is an intentional stylistic device supported by story, sound, movement, or a clear reset.

### NO_EMPTY_ESTABLISHING
- Do not: Use a wide establishing shot that contains no story tension, product context, or mood information.
- Why: It slows the edit without helping orientation.
- Fix: Make the wide carry obstacle, scale, location value, before-state, or social proof.
- Exception: Only when it is an intentional stylistic device supported by story, sound, movement, or a clear reset.

## Shot Size Transition Matrix

- ELS to LS/MS: Safe when context is needed. ELS to CU: only with a strong sound or meaning bridge.
- LS to MS/FS: Standard orientation. LS to ECU: needs an insert motivator.
- MS to MCU/CU/insert: Usually the safest UGC transition.
- CU to MCU/MS: Safe exit from emotion. CU to ELS: not by default; needs reveal, shock, comedy, or loneliness.
- ECU/macro to CU/insert: Safe. ECU to wide: only if the wide reveals what the viewer was seeing.
- INSERT works as a bridge between almost any shot sizes when tied to action, sound, shape, or meaning.

## Mood Blocks

### trust_expert
- Sequence: MS expert anchor to insert proof to CU calm face to screen/product demo to wide context.
- Best for: apps_tech, beauty, pet_products.
- Avoid: Over-stylized moves, shaky camera, fake urgency.

### problem_solution
- Sequence: chaos wide to CU frustration to failed object insert to solution reveal MS to proof insert to reaction CU.
- Best for: apps_tech, fashion, pet_products.
- Avoid: Showing the product before the problem is felt.

### sensory_food
- Sequence: macro texture to hands action to steam/pour motion to bite reaction to hero plate.
- Best for: food_beverage.
- Avoid: Too many overheads with no face or reaction.

### luxury_premium
- Sequence: slow macro to negative-space wide to controlled glide to hands detail to held hero.
- Best for: beauty, fashion, food_beverage.
- Avoid: Handheld chaos, jump cuts, cluttered backgrounds.

### street_raw
- Sequence: street wide to live reaction to moving two-shot to detail insert to direct CTA.
- Best for: fashion, food_beverage, social proof.
- Avoid: Over-clean studio polish.
