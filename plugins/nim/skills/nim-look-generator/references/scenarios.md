# Practical Scenario Map

Match the meaning of a request, not an isolated keyword. Matching works semantically across languages, including translated phrases, transliterations, synonyms, and differences in capitalization; all examples here are in English. These are representative phrases, not required commands. The user's current choices take precedence over the previous brief, presets, and defaults; follow-up requests preserve everything the user has not changed. Do not turn this table into a questionnaire.

This map describes decisions that affect the user's result. See [brief-and-controls.md](brief-and-controls.md) for control semantics, [model-routing.md](model-routing.md) for generator selection, and [nim-workflow.md](nim-workflow.md) for execution and result handling.

All new visual series begin with the mandatory **Recraft V4.1 Pro casting portrait** under [face-first.md](face-first.md). This applies to the rows below even when only the clothing decision is described. One character is reused throughout a series and its continuations; text-only and explicit identity/portrait overrides follow that reference. The portrait is a separate asset, never one of the requested looks.

| No. | Intent and natural phrases | Interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | “Make a look,” “Put together an outfit and show it,” “Generate a fashion look” | A visualization is requested; no style or person is specified. | Cast a distinctive fictional adult through Recraft V4.1 Pro first, then use the completed portrait to show one wearable free-style outfit on that person, with all optional controls off, on a 16:9 lookbook sheet. Briefly state material assumptions. Do not ask about every setting or choose an arbitrary preset. |
| 2 | “What should I wear with these trousers?”, “Evaluate this combination,” “How would you style this?” | Clothing advice, not permission to run paid image generation. | Give a concrete combination of garments, colors, fit, and ways to wear them; explain the key choices. Use the photo if it is available. Do not upload references to Nim or generate images. |
| 3 | “Prompts only,” “No generation,” “Three ideas first,” “Prompt text only,” “Don't render” | `delivery=prompts` or written ideas; this limits the requested deliverable. | Return the requested number of ready-to-use prompts or ideas, with clear reference roles when needed. Do not upload or generate. If the user later says “Now generate the second one,” use that selected option without repeating the intake. |
| 4 | “Select Prompt only in the style setting,” “Use Prompt only mode and generate” | The style setting means `styleMode=free`, without preset cues. It does not itself set `delivery=prompts`. | Use only the user's wishes, required items, and references; proceed with visualization when requested. “Prompt only style mode, but give me just the prompt text” sets both free styling and text delivery. If the bare phrase “prompt only” does not refer to the interface setting, interpret it as text without generation. |
| 5 | “General style: Office,” “For the beach,” “General: Street style,” “Cozy clothes for home” | A general group supplies context and a range of variations, not a single fixed costume. The available groups are **Cozy home, Street style, Office, Gym, Evening, Beach, Casual**. | Read the selected group in the style library and choose a coherent direction within it. For multiple looks, use suitable distinct directions while retaining the constraints. Evening and Beach do not enable Sexy; no group automatically adds prohibited accessories. |
| 6 | “Specifically Gorpcore,” “Old money outfit,” “Y2K, without mixing styles” | A specific preset, not a general group. The available presets are **Gorpcore, Old money, Clean girl, Y2K, Downtown NYC, Scandi minimal, Blokecore**. | Read the matching entry and express it through concrete fabrics, cuts, colors, and details. Do not substitute a neighboring style. The label Clean girl does not assign a person's gender presentation or identity. |
| 7 | “Minimalism with Japanese proportions,” “For a gallery, without office formality,” “My own style, no presets” | A free direction outside the menu, or an explicit decision not to use presets. | Turn the description into 2–4 concrete clothing decisions. Do not force the request into the nearest menu option. If an unfamiliar term materially affects the result, clarify that term alone and retain the rest of the brief. |
| 8 | “70% Scandi minimal, 30% Gorpcore,” “Take the colors from this image and the texture from that one,” “Inspired by these images” | Soft inspiration or a weighted blend, not a requirement to reproduce every object in an image. | Assign each source a role. Use the dominant style for the silhouette and palette, with the secondary style providing a limited accent. Percentages express artistic priority, not measurable pixel similarity. When appropriate, inspect soft references and translate them into written cues; do not promote a face from an inspiration image into an identity reference. |
| 9 | “Change my outfit in this photo,” “Same person, new look,” “Keep my face” | The photo explicitly defines identity; this overrides fictional casting. | Preserve the face, skin tone, apparent age, build, proportions, and hair. Specify the permitted changes. Choose a compatible reference/edit workflow and inspect the finished result; do not promise absolute identity fidelity. Do not create a replacement Recraft face or combine a new fictional face with the selected identity. |
| 10 | “Here are photos of me from different angles,” “Use these two portraits” | Multiple identity references may show the same person or different people. | If they clearly represent one specified person, use the photos together as identity anchors. If the target person is unclear, ask one question about which identity to use. Do not average faces or blend identities unless requested; count every required photo when selecting the model. |
| 11 | “Clothes only, no person,” “Outfit only,” “Ghost mannequin,” “No hands” | `subjectMode=outfit-only`; the outfit images must contain no person. | Keep the initial Recraft portrait as a separate casting asset and disclose it in the scope/cost. Omit its identity input from outfit jobs. Exclude people, visible mannequins, skin, faces, hands, hair, and grooming from the clothing output. An explicit “no portraits at all” overrides casting as well. |
| 12 | “Show it on a model,” “On a new adult model,” “Not on me” | A new fictional adult person is needed; a selfie is not required. | Generate a distinctive face through Recraft V4.1 Pro, inspect it, and use its real image as the identity anchor in a compatible outfit edit model. Do not take identity from product or inspiration photos. Use any specified age and appearance; when the choice is open, state the adult casting assumption. |
| 13 | “Build the outfit around this exact jacket,” “These boots and the bag in the photo,” “Use the exact garment” | The garment photo is a required product reference; text about the same item clarifies its use. | Preserve each item's cut, color, material, construction, and distinctive details. Several views of one item still represent one item; the phrase “the jacket in the photo” does not create a second jacket. Do not transfer the product photo model's face into the result. Do not replace an exact photo with a written description to fit an input limit. |
| 14 | “Only these items,” “No accessories,” “Remove all bags,” “No extras” | A closed inventory or explicit category exclusion, stronger than switching a control off. | Do not add a belt, socks, headwear, jewelry, or props that contradict the inventory. A dress or jumpsuit does not require a second garment to fill out the outfit; a pair of shoes normally counts as one item. A newer removal request supersedes an earlier requirement to keep the item; simultaneous instructions to “keep and remove” require a focused clarification. |
| 15 | “Bag ON,” “Bag is off, but keep my bag,” “Add one more bag” | The switch governs inventing an additional bag; a specific user-required bag exists independently. | ON adds exactly one new bag beyond the required items; OFF does not remove the user's bag. “Add a bag” creates one item, without a second automatic addition. “One more” means one additional bag, not both a new item and another activation of the control. |
| 16 | “Outerwear ON,” “One more outer layer,” “Drape the coat over the shoulders” | An additional wearable layer beyond the required clothes; the user may specify how it is worn. | With ON, add exactly one visible layer, worn over the existing clothing by default. Preserve the original garment and its visibility. Do not replace it with a coat or move the layer into a hand without a request. Explicit “over the shoulders / around the waist / held in the hands” instructions determine handling; discuss physically incompatible layering before generation. |
| 17 | “Props ON,” “Put a book in the hand,” “No scene props” | A prop is a separate object used in the scene or action, not required clothing. | ON allows exactly one suitable additional prop. An explicitly requested book is one specific item, without another added by the control. OFF stops inventing props; “remove all props” excludes them. In outfit-only mode, do not create a hand just to hold the book. |
| 18 | “Jewelry ON,” “Keep this ring,” “No jewelry” | Additional jewelry, required jewelry, and a category exclusion are different constraints. | With ON, invent exactly one additional piece of jewelry; with OFF, retain only jewelry specified by the user. “No jewelry” excludes the category from the current look. Do not treat a preset suggestion as permission to add a chain, earrings, and a bracelet together. |
| 19 | “Styling ON,” “Make it more interesting through the fit,” “Styling OFF, but roll up the sleeves” | How existing clothing is worn; the inventory remains closed. | With ON, choose two compatible techniques: a visible change to the overall composition and a supporting detail in another area. Name the garment, area, extent of the change, and visual effect; keep each technique within 200 characters. For a dress, use two areas of that garment. OFF does not cancel the explicit sleeve request. If handling is locked, describe existing features without altering them. |
| 20 | “Print ON,” “Print OFF,” “No prints,” “Keep the striped shirt” | ON requires a visible print on at least one eligible garment in every look; OFF only removes that obligation. “No prints” is a separate exclusion. | With ON, define the motif, scale, placement, and colors. Do not print on a locked plain garment without permission. Preserve the specified striped shirt when Print is OFF; resolve the conflict if the user also prohibits all patterns. Stripes, checks, lettering, and monograms count as patterns; ordinary knit texture is not itself a print. |
| 21 | “Sexy ON,” “More sensual, but keep it covered,” “Less sexualized,” “An outfit for a teenager” | Clothing presentation and permitted coverage; age restrictions apply. | For a clearly adult character, use non-explicit fit, draping, materials, and permitted necklines without changing the body or adding nudity. “Covered / no sexualization” constrains the result. For a minor or unclear age, use an ordinary age-appropriate outfit. Sexy OFF does not cancel a separately requested permissible silhouette. |
| 22 | “Keep the hair as it is,” “Give me a short bob,” “Change the hair color,” “Change the hairstyle” | With an identity reference, hair stays unchanged unless the user explicitly requests a change in any language. | Apply only the named change to length, styling, or color; preserve the face, body, and unspecified attributes. “More natural” alone does not authorize a new hairstyle. Do not add a head to outfit-only output to accommodate a hair setting. |
| 23 | “Masculine, but keep the skirt,” “Feminine tailoring,” “Neutral, with makeup” | Presentation describes clothing construction and proportions; it does not define sex, identity, or body shape. | Preserve skirts, dresses, leggings, and all other required garments with any presentation. Do not alter the body or infer ethnicity or identity. Do not invent makeup by default for neutral/masculine presentation, but follow an explicit makeup request. |
| 24 | “An eight-piece capsule for the week,” “Five outfits from one capsule” | One limited shared wardrobe, with several combinations that reuse items. | First define a single inventory and stable descriptions of the items, then map combinations to days or occasions. Do not create five independent wardrobes or count a repeated item as a new piece. Clarify only a real conflict between the item count and required inventory. |
| 25 | “One full-body frame,” “Full-body, shoes visible,” “Top-down flat lay” | Framing and item visibility, not the number of distinct outfits. | Default to 9:16 for one full-body frame and 1:1 for a flat lay; the user's explicit format takes precedence. Show the head and shoes without cropping when a full-body person is requested. For a flat lay, arrange the whole outfit without a person; do not substitute the standard 16:9 sheet. |
| 26 | “An outfit sheet,” “The same look from the front, side, and back,” “Three views, separate files” | One inventory may have several views; one sheet and several files are different deliverables. | Preserve construction, items, colors, hair, and identity between views. Do not turn three views into three new outfits. Produce one sheet or separate images as requested; use a sheet when the layout is unspecified. Check both the number of views and the number of files. |
| 27 | “Remove the bag in the second one,” “Make this jacket red,” “Same image, different background” | A local edit to a selected result; a new explicit edit may override the previous lock on that particular attribute. | Use the selected finished image as the base. Change only the bag, jacket color, or background respectively; do not rebuild the whole look. An authorized color change preserves the garment's other construction details. If “the second one” is ambiguous, clarify the option; if the base image is still generating, wait for it. |
| 28 | “Three more, only change the shoes,” “Same style, different silhouettes,” “Keep everything except the jacket” | Controlled variation with locked and permitted dimensions of change. | Retain every lock and vary only the specified dimensions. Prepare separate concrete prompts for distinct outfits. Do not treat random seeds as a guarantee of different silhouettes, and do not change locked identity, colors, or clothing. |
| 29 | “Make six outfits,” “Ten variations of this outfit” | The requested count may exceed a single request's capacity; distinct outfits differ from random takes of one outfit. | Use one casting portrait for the series unless different people are requested. Produce exactly the requested outfit count after casting, retaining numbering and the overall budget. Ten distinct outfit images require eleven image jobs with the default one portrait. Track distinct outfit prompts separately from variations of one prompt; never count the portrait as an outfit. |
| 30 | “Choose the model yourself,” “Flux2 only,” “Use Recraft V4.1,” “Use another Nim model” | The current assistant writes prompts and calls Nim; casting uses Recraft V4.1 Pro and outfits default to Nano Banana Pro / Pro Edit. Explicit stage/model choices take precedence. | Check the intended stage and live compatibility. “Flux for outfits” keeps the Recraft portrait and allows Flux 2 Edit with its reference. “Only Flux for every stage” explicitly overrides the casting model. Do not silently use text-only Recraft to preserve a generated identity, or standard Recraft as the Pro portrait. Resolve materially ambiguous scope or an incompatible exact model before spending. |
| 31 | “My face plus three exact garments, 4:5, 4K,” “Do not change the format” | Every required photo, aspect ratio, and resolution is a simultaneous hard constraint. | Find a model that satisfies all of them. Do not remove photos, secretly combine them, or treat the text “4K” as actual support. If no compatible solution exists, agree on a specific compromise involving format, resolution, or a separate processing step. Do not replace exact product/identity photos with an inspirational description. |
| 32 | “Use the file at this path,” “Use the photo in the chat,” “Try it on me” without an accessible photo | A required reference may be visible as a preview but unavailable for transfer, or the source may be missing. | Check whether the actual file or a supported source is accessible. If not, request only the missing file or an accessible path. If upload is blocked, explain the concrete cause and required environment fix; do not run a text-only substitute, invent a reference URL, or claim that the image has already been uploaded. |
| 33 | “Is it ready?”, “It's taking a long time,” “Still running?”, “Show the status” | A status request for an existing job, not a new generation request. | Check the existing jobs and report their actual status. Continue waiting for existing requests; exceeding the typical duration alone does not mean failure. Do not describe a queued request as a finished result or create a duplicate because of impatience or an elapsed waiting period. |
| 34 | “Three of five are ready,” “This option failed,” “Retry only the failed one,” “Retry within this budget” | A partial result or an explicitly authorized limited retry. | Deliver finished options with their original numbering and identify failures separately. Retry only selected options or those already authorized for correction, within the stated limit; do not rerun successful jobs. Without permission to iterate, do not conceal a new paid run behind an error message. Insufficient credits are not a reason to retry a job. |
| 35 | “Export the prompts and settings,” “Save a PNG,” “Download all results” | An actual export of specific available content, not a promise of persistent storage. | Create the requested local files using available means, or provide real finished download links. For a text export, save the brief, prompts, model names, and parameters; exclude secrets and temporary upload tokens. Do not present a URL as a downloaded file. State format and download limitations plainly; follow the workflow's media delivery rules. |
| 36 | “Favorite the second and fourth,” “Let's come back to this look later,” “Save this as a favorite” | Selection of options for follow-up; a persistent server gallery is not automatically implied. | Record the selected options and their settings in the current context. If continuity across sessions is needed, save a real local card/list using available means and provide its path. Do not claim that a Nim favorite or external database record exists without an available tool and a confirmed write. |
| 37 | “Enlarge the second result,” “Make it 2×,” “Upscale without redesign” | A separate operation that increases the size of a finished image, not a new creative generation. | Use the selected finished image and the available Nim upscale workflow. Preserve the design, resolve only unsupported requirements, and account for the cost. Do not upscale automatically to compensate for unsupported initial 4K, or describe changing a resolution setting as processing an existing file. |
| 38 | “No more than 20 credits,” “The cheapest option,” “I've run out of credits” | An explicit spending limit applies to casting plus all outfits and authorized corrections. | Check the whole pipeline before paying for the portrait. A portrait currently estimated at 40 credits cannot fit a complete 20-credit order; do not submit it or silently omit/downgrade it. Propose a concrete change for the user to choose. Cheapest outfit routing does not change the mandatory portrait model. Do not buy credits or change a subscription automatically. |
| 39 | “Generate through Nim” when tools are unavailable; “I can't authenticate” | The requested generation is blocked by connection or access; a text fallback is a different deliverable. | Identify the concrete blocker. Prepare everything available without cost, including the brief and prompts, but do not declare the order complete. Do not silently switch providers. Provide the text draft as interim work; finish with a prompt-only result only when the user has chosen that fallback. |
| 40 | “No logos or lettering,” “Natural skin, no watermark,” “No wear marks; this is a new garment” | Explicit exclusions and realism should protect visible details without conflicting with required references. | Include brief, specific constraints in the prompt; do not invent unsupported model parameters. Preserve natural fabric drape and suitable texture without artificial wear on new merchandise. If an exact required item already contains a prohibited logo or print, clarify permission to remove it. Inspect the result visually; do not promise that written negatives guarantee artifact-free output. |
| 41 | “Start a new lookbook,” “A new collection with a fresh model,” “Surprise me with the face” | A new series with no preserved identity requires fresh casting. | First generate one original face through Recraft V4.1 Pro. Vary a coherent set of visible traits from accessible prior castings. Use natural anatomy and texture; do not copy a fixed face prompt or promise global uniqueness. |
| 42 | “Ten looks on ten different people,” “Cast three different models” | Distinct identities are explicitly requested. | Plan one portrait per character, with separate trait briefs and mappings. Complete casting before outfit generation. Include every portrait in the total cost; do not use random variations of one face prompt as a substitute for planned casting diversity. |
| 43 | “Use that same model for three more looks,” “Retry look four,” “Show the face again” | Reuse the established identity and completed casting asset. | Keep the accepted portrait and successful outfits. Submit only requested new/failed outfit work within the authorized limit; showing an existing portrait requires no generation. Existing results remain valid even without a recorded Recraft casting job. |
| 44 | “Use the new model with this exact jacket photo” | Generated portrait plus one mandatory product photo means two inputs. | Preflight both references before casting. Use Nano Banana Pro Edit with both references when the live contract supports the format and input count; its checked maximum is 14 images. A requested Flux fallback may fail its one-image limit. Preserve both the face and the jacket. Do not omit an input, merge references silently, or use text-only generation. |
| 45 | “The portrait is still queued,” “Casting failed,” “The face is cropped” | Dependent outfit jobs cannot begin from a pending or unusable portrait. | Track the original job, inspect actual media, and follow bounded correction rules. Do not fabricate a portrait URL, submit a duplicate while status is uncertain, or bypass casting with a face description. Keep successful assets if later outfits fail. |
| 46 | “No portraits at all,” “Skip the face step,” “Prompts only for the face and looks” | A specific override of portrait generation or of all paid generation. | Record the override and follow the narrower request. Prompt-only output may include a future-reference placeholder but no invented image URL or paid jobs. An explicit skip is different from a flat-lay format, which normally retains casting as a separate asset. |

## Activation Boundaries

“Chop an onion for a salad,” “Fix the look of this button,” “Interior styling,” and location-only images are outside this skill's scope. If the skill is explicitly invoked but no fashion task is present, ask one focused question about the subject.

“Where can I buy this / find the price / order these boots” requires a separate product search or shopping workflow: do not invent stores, availability, prices, or completed purchases. Route “Animate this lookbook / make a video” to an available Nim video workflow only when explicitly requested; do not promise a finished video from an image-only workflow.

For follow-ups such as “Do it as before,” use the brief and selected outputs that are actually available. If the context has been lost, identify the specific option or file needed; do not invent previous settings or claim a saved history without an actual record.
