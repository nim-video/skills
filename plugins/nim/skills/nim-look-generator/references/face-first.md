# Face First: Recraft V4.1 Pro Casting

## Required order and scope

For every new visual look series, generate an original, distinctive face through **Recraft V4.1 Pro before generating any outfits**. This stage is required even when no person was mentioned. Default to one fictional adult wearing the looks. Brief preparation, reference inspection, catalog discovery, and total-cost checks may happen first; the portrait must be the first generative job.

One series uses one character by default. Ten different outfits mean one casting portrait plus ten outfit outputs, not ten portraits. If the user requests ten different people, plan ten separate character briefs and portraits, complete casting before the outfit stage, and map each look to its assigned identity. Count the portraits as additional assets and paid jobs, never as completed outfits.

- **New series / new character:** cast a fresh face. Vary its design from accessible previous castings; do not reuse the previous image or a fixed template seed.
- **Continuation / edit / retry:** reuse the established identity. "Three more," "same person," a rear view, a background change, status, export, and upscale do not start another casting job. Existing images remain valid identity/base references even without a recorded casting job; do not recast them to manufacture a missing history.
- **Advice / prompts / no generation:** no portrait jobs or uploads. In prompts mode, provide the casting prompt and dependent outfit prompts where relevant, describing the future identity reference as a placeholder, never as an existing URL.
- **Explicit identity override:** "use my face," "keep this person," or an explicitly selected existing character uses that identity instead of fictional casting. Do not replace or blend it with a generated person. Recraft's text-only portrait model cannot reproduce an exact supplied identity. Record `identityOrigin=user-reference` or `existing-character` and the override.
- **Outfit-only / flat lay / ghost:** still create the initial portrait as a separate casting asset, but do not pass it into the person-free outfit jobs or introduce skin, faces, hands, or hair into them. Clearly state this separate asset in the initial cost/scope summary. A more specific "no portraits at all" or "skip face generation" overrides the casting step.
- **Explicit model override:** "use Flux for the outfits" changes only outfit routing. A request selecting a different face model or "only Flux for every stage" overrides this standing preference; record the change. If the model's intended stage is materially ambiguous, resolve it before spending.

When the user explicitly skips casting and no existing identity is available, create the first person-bearing outfit with a compatible text-input model, normally Nano Banana Pro. Inspect that completed full-body result and use it as the image identity reference for subsequent looks; do not invent a missing portrait URL or call an image-input endpoint without a reference. Record `identityOrigin=first-outfit` and the explicit skip. This is an override path, never an automatic shortcut. If a requested exact product already requires image input, use the compatible product-reference workflow for the first outfit and then preserve its newly established person.

These are explicit delivery/identity overrides and continuation rules, not permission to omit casting to save time, reduce cost, satisfy an input limit, or compensate for an unavailable model. Without such an override, an unavailable Recraft V4.1 Pro or unaffordable complete pipeline is a blocker. Present the concrete alternative before submitting a paid job.

## Design a distinctive face

The current assistant designs the face and writes the prompt; Recraft renders it. Create a concise casting brief with an explicit adult age, a coherent combination of 4–6 visible facial characteristics, hair, skin detail, expression, and framing. Respect user-specified appearance; choose unspecified traits creatively rather than requiring a casting questionnaire. Clothing presentation does not determine ethnicity, gender, or body shape. Explicit requests for a younger subject are handled as age-appropriate, nonsexual fashion; the adult default must not silently override a stated age.

Use varied facial structure and proportions, such as an elongated or broad face, softly angular jaw, high or low cheekbones, brow shape, eye spacing, a defined or gently curved nose, a characteristic lip contour, subtle asymmetry, or natural freckles. Choose a few mutually coherent details. Do not impose the same narrow nose, large lips, perfect symmetry, porcelain skin, or youth on every character. A distinctive face does not require an exaggerated defect, heavy cosmetics, a costume, or a celebrity resemblance. Do not infer personality from anatomy or promise that the face is globally unique.

Use a photorealistic casting portrait by default: one person, complete head and chin, face large enough to inspect, front-facing or a slight three-quarter angle with both eyes visible, hairline unobscured, neutral expression, relaxed shoulders, plain background, and even soft light. Preserve natural skin texture. No sunglasses, face-obscuring hands, hats, heavy glamour retouching, beauty filters, labels, or watermarks unless explicitly required. A plain crewneck top keeps the portrait non-explicit; it is incidental casting clothing and **not** a required outfit item.

Example casting prompt — vary the traits for each new casting, do not copy this face repeatedly:

```text
Create a photorealistic fashion casting portrait of one original fictional adult, age 34. A long oval face with a softly angular jaw, high cheekbones, a gently convex nose bridge, wide-set hazel eyes beneath straight full brows, and a subtle natural asymmetry in the upper lip. Warm medium skin with visible pores and a few light freckles across the nose. Short dark wavy hair, softly irregular hairline, no makeup. Calm neutral expression, direct gaze, nearly frontal view with both eyes clearly visible. Show the complete head, hairline, chin, neck and relaxed shoulders; the face occupies most of the frame. Plain charcoal crewneck top, pale gray background, soft balanced studio lighting, realistic color. Distinctive natural anatomy, without exaggerated features or glamour retouching. One person, no collage, no text, logos, watermark, jewelry or props.
```

## Discover, generate, bind

The exact Recraft selection below applies to the standing default. For an explicit different-face-model override, discover that model and obey its own contract throughout the same dependency sequence; record `identityOrigin=other-cast` and the actual model rather than claiming a Recraft origin. Existing-identity and explicit no-casting paths use their documented override instead of running these portrait steps.

1. Search the live Nim catalog for **Recraft V4.1 Pro**, match the exact display name, and get its final contract. Do not select Recraft V4.1 standard because it appears nearby or is cheaper. Never freeze a model UUID in the Skill.
2. Preflight downstream models with the future portrait counted as one identity image for each person-bearing look. Verify all mandatory references, requested outfit formats, and the total estimate before generating the portrait. Check credit balance because this is a Pro stage. Outfit model preference and outfit format are separate from portrait settings.
3. At the verified 2026-09-09 contract, the portrait uses `requestedAspectRatio="3:4"`, `resolution="2K"`, and `batchSize=1`; omit `seed` to randomize unless the user requests one. These are defaults subject to the live contract, not permanent API constants. `fileInputs` is forbidden for this text-only model. Pass its discovered `model_id`, `model_name`, and the casting prompt. If the portrait settings cease to be supported, choose other supported portrait framing/resolution within the user's constraints; do not replace the required model silently.
4. Save the returned job IDs and poll to terminal status. A queued, failed, missing-media, or uncertain portrait cannot serve as an identity input. Do not submit outfits in parallel with unfinished casting. Use the bounded recovery rules in [nim-workflow.md](nim-workflow.md).
5. Inspect the completed portrait: one face, requested age/traits, natural anatomy, usable framing and lighting, unobscured features, and no unwanted text. A vague desire for more "uniqueness" does not authorize repeated paid attempts. A clear defect follows the bounded repair policy. If the portrait is unusable or inspection is unavailable, retain the real result and state the specific limitation; do not call it an approved identity or proceed blindly into dependent outfit jobs.
6. Bind the usable `mediaUrl` to the series as `identityOrigin=recraft-cast` for an actual Recraft V4.1 Pro portrait, or `other-cast` for an explicitly overridden portrait model. Save a short description of the **observed** face and hair, correcting deviations from the planned casting brief. Keep the actual model, prompt, parameters, job IDs/status, and URL with the series; do not save signed upload URLs or credentials.
7. For each new person-bearing outfit, pass this completed portrait as the identity image, then required product images and any necessary inspirations. Use **Nano Banana Pro Edit** when compatible, for the portrait alone or together with required products. The current assistant assembles the outfit prompt; Nano Banana Pro Edit renders it. Do not reupload an accepted Nim URL. Prompt explicitly to preserve the portrait's face, age, skin tone, hair, and visible proportions while replacing its incidental clothing with the planned outfit; do not copy its shoulder crop or backdrop into a full-body scene.

The portrait alone does not establish full-body proportions. Choose a stable, ordinary body description consistent with any explicit user requirements; do not claim it was observed in the headshot. Once a full-body look exists, preserve its body proportions in follow-ups. If an edit requires both that base and the original face, count both inputs and route compatibly. A base-only edit may preserve its established identity when the requested change is local; the original casting remains the series identity record.

## Cost, continuity, and output

The full estimate is `number of new castings × current portrait cost + all outfit jobs + authorized corrections/operations`. Use the current portrait estimate. For example, do not start a 40-credit portrait for a complete order capped at 20 credits. Do not silently switch to standard Recraft or omit the face to meet that cap.

Deliver or make the casting portrait accessible separately from the numbered looks, subject to the host's media rules. For person-bearing outputs, inspect facial structure, hair and skin tone against the portrait, and the same identity across views and outfits. API completion alone does not prove identity fidelity. If an outfit fails, retain the accepted portrait and all successful looks; retry only the affected job within the authorized limit.
